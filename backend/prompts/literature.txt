LITERATURE INTERPRETATION GUIDANCE

When synthesizing literature evidence, apply the following principles:

1. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
   - Systematic reviews and meta-analyses carry the highest weight
   - Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) provide strong causal evidence
   - Observational studies suggest associations but not causality
   - In vitro and animal studies are hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory
   - Case reports and case series are signals only

2. TEMPORAL CONTEXT
   - Note when evidence is recent (last 2 years) versus older
   - Flag if the field has evolved significantly over time
   - Acknowledge when foundational studies are old and replication is limited

3. CONSISTENCY
   - Note when multiple independent studies agree
   - Explicitly flag contradictory findings
   - Distinguish between different study populations, doses, or conditions

4. BIOLOGICAL PLAUSIBILITY
   - Connect molecular mechanisms to clinical observations where evidence allows
   - Avoid over-extrapolating from cell or animal models to humans

5. AUTHORSHIP AND SOURCE DIVERSITY
   - Note if evidence comes primarily from a single research group
   - Independent replication strengthens confidence in findings

Use these principles to frame literature summaries without editorializing beyond the supplied evidence.
